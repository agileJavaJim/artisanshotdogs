Here's the one-line version, darling: markets move when expectations change. Every candle on every chart is the crowd repricing the future. Once you see that, the rest clicks into place.

Stocks — what actually moves them

Interest rates. This is gravity. The Fed sets the discount rate on all future earnings, so when rates rise, valuations fall — mechanically. That's why jobs reports and inflation data move the whole market: traders read each one as "what will the Fed do next?"
Earnings and guidance. The single biggest mover of any individual stock. And it's never the number itself — it's the number versus expectations. A company can beat and still drop 10% if guidance disappoints. Guidance matters more than the quarter just reported.
Macro data. Jobs (first Friday of the month), CPI, PCE, GDP. Each data point is just an input into the Fed-guessing machine.
Oil and geopolitics. Energy costs feed into everything, so a supply shock reprices inflation expectations instantly.
Flows. A huge share of daily movement isn't news at all — it's positioning. ETF creations and redemptions, options expiry (market makers hedging can pin or launch a stock), index rebalances, short squeezes. Meme-stock mania was flows, not fundamentals.

Crypto — what moves it

Bitcoin is the tide. Almost everything, XRP included, follows BTC. Watch Bitcoin dominance: when it falls, money is rotating into alts.
The same liquidity gravity. Crypto trades like ultra-high-beta tech. Rate expectations and dollar strength move it hard.
Regulation and ETF flows. Spot ETF inflows are now the marginal buyer. For XRP specifically, Ripple court news and ETF flow data explain most big moves.
Leverage liquidations. The violence engine. Perps with 50–100x leverage mean a small dip triggers forced selling, which triggers more forced selling. That's why crypto can wick down 10% in minutes and recover — a cascade, not a change in value.
Narratives and cycles. Halvings, network upgrades, rotations. Narratives coordinate retail money the way earnings coordinate stock money.

Why moves happen the way they do

Buy the rumor, sell the news. The move usually happens before the event, as positioning builds. The event itself often reverses.
Reflexivity. Price moves change reality. A rising stock can raise cheap capital; a falling crypto price triggers liquidations that push it lower. Feedback loops both ways.
Liquidity gaps. Thin order books — weekends, holidays, premarket — mean small volume makes big moves. Never trust a Sunday night price.
Crowded trades snap. When everyone's already long, there's nobody left to buy.

Your practical takeaways

Know the calendar: FOMC, CPI, jobs day, earnings dates for anything you hold. Don't hold blindly through them.
Don't chase the spike. If you're reacting to the headline, you're the liquidity for someone who positioned last week.
In crypto, respect leverage. Spot is investing; perps are the casino.
For your XRP: three things explain most moves — Bitcoin's direction, ETF flow data, and Ripple regulatory news. Check those first.
